Ascent Assistant · mobile redesign

Two ways to make the phone app feel finished

Same brand, same two screens, two different opinions about how a phone should carry them. Everything below is a static picture — nothing is wired to JobTread. Scroll inside each phone; flip each option between light and dark with the switch above it.

What doesn't change

Cream #FAF7EE, off-black #1B1B17, ochre #CF9803, olive #878054, web grey #8D8D8B, the mountain-square logo, and Roboto. Ochre still never carries small text — it's fills, rules and active states only, exactly as the brand guide has it. Both options keep the light = cream + ochre / dark = off-black + olive pairing.

What both options fix

  • Money reads from arm's length. One headline figure per screen, set in tabular figures at display size, with the approved/draft split demoted to a line underneath.
  • State is a shape, not a sentence. Draft, no-file, invoiced, over-budget each get their own colour and mark, so a list is scannable without reading.
  • Budget headroom comes back on the phone. Today the cost-code rail is collapsed on mobile; both options bring it back as something you swipe, not something you unfold.
  • Fewer taps to a page. Today every area is a closed accordion — three taps to reach Client Invoicing from a cold start.
Option A

Ledger

The site binder, tidied. Everything is a hairline-divided list on a cream ground; the only ornament is a 2px ochre rule marking a section or a number. Nothing folds shut — you scroll instead of hunting, with a search field for the twenty-odd pages. Conservative: it's your current app with the noise removed, so it ships fast and nothing about it needs relearning.

What Option A changes, concretely

  • The job you're on gets its own row under the logo, with the address — instead of being squeezed into a truncated picker.
  • Areas stop being accordions. Financials, HR, Utilities and Admin are open lists; the long ones show three and offer "show N more". Search covers the rest.
  • Quick actions shrink from four 92px boxes to a 4-across icon rail, which is what pushes the real content above the fold.
  • Invoicing leads with the statement block — ochre rule, figure, split, then one plain sentence about what JobTread will actually pull.
  • Filters become chips in a swipe row, so the four toggles stop owning a quarter of the screen.
  • Bills get a 3px status stripe — amber draft, blue invoiced, red over-budget — so you can scan a month without reading a word.
  • The Revert / Sync bar only appears when something is staged, and sticks to the bottom when it does.
Option B

Console

A proper phone app rather than a launcher. Every screen opens with an off-black hero panel — brand-dark, mountain watermarked — that carries the one number or the one alert that matters, and the working content sits below it on cream. A fixed bottom tab bar replaces "everything goes through home". Bolder, and a bigger change to how you move around.

What Option B changes, concretely

  • A bottom tab bar — Home, Invoicing, Time, More — so the launcher stops being the only way between pages. This is the biggest departure and the biggest win in daily taps.
  • The home screen answers "what needs me?" before it offers a menu: the alert counts and the month's figure sit in the hero, not in banners you scroll past.
  • Areas become four tiles that open a sheet, with their pages previewed on the tile — half the height of four accordion rows.
  • Invoicing's hero carries the number plus a stacked meter showing approved vs draft against the budget, which the current page has nowhere on mobile.
  • Bills group by day with a day subtotal, so a month reads as a timeline instead of one long list — and each bill shows its vendor initials as a colour-coded status mark.
  • The action bar is always docked above the tabs when changes are staged, with the "nothing is written until you sync" reassurance right on it.

What I need from you

  1. Pick a direction — A, B, or a mix (for example: A's Invoicing screen with B's tab bar is entirely buildable).
  2. Say what's wrong. Any element here can move, shrink, or go. The figures, vendor names and cost codes are invented placeholders — real data slots straight in.
  3. Then I build it on the claude/mobile-ui-redesign-mockups-m2bdye branch, push, and Vercel gives you a preview URL to open on your phone before anything reaches production.
